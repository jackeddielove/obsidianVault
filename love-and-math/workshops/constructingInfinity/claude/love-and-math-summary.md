## The Business Concept
A combined **math tutoring center and makerspace**, with mathematics as the core differentiator.

**Two audiences, two revenue streams:**
- Tutoring (K-12, after school) — existing client base, proven model
- Makerspace classes and workshops (kids and adults) — new audience, broader reach

**Why the combination works:**
- Shared space, complementary scheduling
- Math becomes visceral and fun through making (CNC geometry, 3D printing, laser-cut tessellations, circuits)
- Tutoring families are a natural first audience for workshops
- Adult makerspace draws weekend and evening traffic

## The Summer Workshop: *Constructing Infinity*
A two-session workshop (split across two weeks, ~2 hours each) exploring infinite ordinals and real projective space.

**Session 1: Counting**
- Warm-up: high-five activity → function notation H(n) = C(n,2)
- Hilbert's Hotel, infinite cardinals, grid path / bijection activity
- Diagonalization argument for uncountability of ℝ ← satisfying stopping point

**Session 2: Building**
- Bridge to projective space via "lines through the origin"
- Picture plane intuition for ℝP¹ and ℝP²
- Video of Boy's surface construction via antipodal identification
- Hands-on assembly of polyhedral Boy's surface model from pre-cut cardstock

**Why two sessions:**
- Prevents rushing the material
- Group reconvenes already knowing each other
- Each session has its own identity and payoff moment

**Format:** ~10 people, borrowed office space (friend's), summer 2025

**Pricing:**
- Tutoring families: charge (~$40-50/person covers $100/hr for yourself plus materials)
- Friends: free or pay-what-you-feel, framed as "beta testers"
- Charging even a small amount improves show rates
- Collect everyone's email — this is the seed of your mailing list

**Logistics:**
- Google Form for RSVPs (even for free attendees)
- Venmo or Stripe for payment
- Personal follow-up reminder the week before
- Have partially-assembled reference models on tables during Boy's surface assembly

## Long-Term Vision

**The grassroots model (your instinct):**
One beautiful, community-embedded space. You know every student. Culture is entirely yours. Financially sustainable through tutoring revenue, workshop fees, and grants. Hire gradually — tutors, shop techs, and ideally former students as teachers and maintainers.

**The scale question (your brother's interest):**
Multi-location, investor-backed. Different business, different life. Means management, standardization, less time doing the thing you love. Not necessary to decide now.

**Key question to sit with:** Is the math and making the point, or is expanding *access* to math and making the point? The answer shapes which path fits.

## The Equity Mission
Inspired by **Bob Moses and the Algebra Project** — mathematical literacy as a civil rights issue.

**Target communities:** Those without access to quality math education — in SF, primarily Black and Latino communities in Bayview, Mission, Visitacion Valley.

**The tension to navigate:**
- Current business serves wealthy families at $175/hr
- Communities you most want to reach are furthest from your current door
- Legitimacy has to be earned through relationships, not assumed

**Possible structures:**
- Cross-subsidy: premium tutoring funds free/sliding-scale community access
- Grant funding: the social justice + maker education framing is genuinely fundable (NEA, NSF, SF education foundations)
- Collaboration model: partner with community-embedded educators rather than positioning yourself as the primary relationship
