I’ve been doing some (basic at this point) reading about ADHD and how it affects math learning specifically and wanted to share some of it to potentially impact how you work with him or other kids with learning differences.  
  
The big thing I’m learning is that the issue isn’t comprehension (which he talks about) — it’s working memory and something called automaticity (basically when practiced things become automatic retrieval). For ADHD brains that process is genuinely slower, which is why he can follow something completely with you and then it evaporates. Repetition alone doesn’t really fix that.  
For the stuff he’s working on right now — scatter plots, graphing, solving for x — a few things that seem to actually help - some of which I know you’ve done with him :
# strategies

- Anchor the concept visually before the procedure — for scatter plots especially, if he understands what the graph is showing (a relationship between two things) before worrying about plotting points, that meaning helps it stick better than just executing steps  
- Written steps he creates himself — not a formula sheet you hand him, but having him write the steps in his own words as you go (which I’ve heard you doing with him - but maybe explaining why you’re doing it that way given how his brain works) so he’s not holding the procedure in working memory while also doing the math  
- For solving for x — externalizing each step on paper as he goes rather than holding intermediate steps in his head. The working memory load of multi-step algebra is significant for ADHD brains  
- Name what he’s actually getting right — his confidence is pretty wrecked around math right now, and there’s a real difference between “I don’t understand this” and “I understand it but can’t retrieve it reliably yet.” Him hearing that distinction from you would mean a lot  
- Check in more frequently within the hour — shorter loops rather than longer stretches, working memory fatigues
