For problem 7, look at the example in the notes that starts off:
![[example1.png]]

Here, we have a power series
$$\frac{1}{1+x}=\sum_{n=0}^\infty (-x)^n$$
which converges on $(-1, 1)$ but diverges at the endpoints. However, when we take the integral
$$\int\frac{1}{1+x}\, dx = \int \sum_{n=0}^\infty \frac{(-x)^n}{n} = \sum_{n=0}^\infty -\frac{(-x)^{n+1}}{n+1}$$
and plug in $x=1$, we get an alternating harmonic series, which converges.
