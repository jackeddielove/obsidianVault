Our common welfare should come first. Personal growth within our relationship depends on unity.

1. Why is our common welfare paramount in a relationship?
	
	 It's a power greater than ourselves. It gives us something to surrender to. Surrender our individual egos. This doesn't mean we give up our identities or our truest sense of who we are as individuals. To the contrary, it builds and supports the growth of those things because we are committed to something greater.
	 
2. What does unity mean to me?
	
	It's a bond. It doesn't mean that we merge. We maintain our separate selves. But there is a bond between us that makes us into something that is bigger than the two of us individually. It is something new, in fact, that we create by making the bond.
	
3. What kind of actions do I take that promote unity in my relationships?
	
	Making plans to spend time together. Doing this work that I'm doing right now for the sake of strengthening that bond.
	
4. What sorts of actions do I take that promote disunity in my relationships?
	
	All of the selfish thinking and worrying and criticizing that I've done. Keeping up a wall, not letting her get too close. Hooking up with other people even when it was "allowed", especially Greer.
	
5. Can I tell the difference between my needs and my desires?
	
	I have a desire to sleep with lots of women. I have a need to share my life with someone. What are my needs? That's a good question.
	
6. What is the connection between Tradition One and Step Three (Made a decision to turn our will and lives over to the care of God as we understood God)?
	
	Kinda like I said in my answer to question 1. We are turning our will and our lives over to the relationship. Again, not that we become consumed or are somehow diminished by giving away who we are. I want to keep reiterating that. But we are trusting that we as individuals will be taken care of when we surrender our will to control the outcome.
	
7. Am I conscious of the difference between my longterm best interest and my short term satisfaction?
	
	This sounds a lot like question 5. I think the main difference is that longterm best interest type stuff is related to growth and nurturing self and others and you might not even know what it's going to look like in the longterm but you have a sense of inspiration that you're going toward something wholesome. Short term satisfaction is just stuff that might make you feel good for a while but doesn't do anything for your soul.
	
8. Which of my codependent behaviors get in the way of unity?
	
	Not aware of how I truly feel. Have difficulty identifying my feelings. Have difficulty expressing emotions. Focus my attention on pleasing the other person. Fear the other person's anger or rejection. Let these fears dictate what I say or how I behave. The first three keep me at a distance from myself, and thus from the relationship. The last three are self-centered and do nothing in service of the relationship.
	
9. Why would I set my own desires aside for the good of a relationship? When might this be necessary?
	
	Same as when I set my desire to go out to the bars aside for the good of my longterm sobriety. It's necessary when they are in direct conflict. 
	
10. What actions do I feel guilty about? Is there a relationship between guilt and feelings of disunity on my part?
	
	I feel guilty about hooking up with coffee shop girl and Greer.  I feel guilty about all the times I told Kara, in one way or another, that she isn't good enough for me. Yeah there's a relationship... this are all things that I did to sabotage our unity, or at least in ignorance of it.
